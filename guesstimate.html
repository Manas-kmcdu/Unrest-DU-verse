What's a guesstimate?

A guesstimate is an estimate you build from scratch, out loud, using only what you already know. Nobody expects you to know how many chai stalls there are near North Campus — but you can reason your way to a number that's in the right neighbourhood, and show exactly how you got there.

It's the core skill tested in consulting and finance case interviews, and it's a useful habit well beyond that: breaking an unfamiliar, unmeasurable question into small pieces you can actually reason about.

  1. Break it down — split the big unknown into smaller, guessable pieces.
  2. Estimate each piece — use whatever you genuinely know: population, rough averages, your own experience.
  3. Put it back together — multiply the pieces back into one number.
  4. Sanity-check the size — does it feel like the right order of magnitude, or off by a factor of ten?
Worked example — how many piano tuners are there in Delhi?
Start from Delhi's population (~3 crore), guess how many households own a piano (maybe 1 in 2,000), and how often each gets tuned (once a year). That's roughly 15,000 tunings a year. If one tuner can do about 500 tunings a year, that's around 30 piano tuners in the city. The exact number matters less than the fact that every step is defensible.

Toolkit

  • Bottom-up decomposition — build the number from small units up: people, then households, then a city.
  • Top-down anchoring — start from a number you're confident about and adjust from there.
  • Round aggressively — 10, 100, 1,000. Precision isn't the point; the chain of logic is.
  • Say your assumptions out loud — a guesstimate is a chain of assumptions, not a lucky number.
